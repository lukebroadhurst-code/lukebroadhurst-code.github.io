Your week

Sound Familiar?

You're running a mechanical crew on a nuclear new build. Nine of you, ten days on, four off.

The nearest towns are full of the project's other contractors. Prices rose while you waited for the programme to confirm. The cheap place still has a long drive and a shuttle bus to the gate.

Door to turnstile, it's longer than the map says. And the rota means beds sit empty on the days off, or people move house at every change-over.

An illustration, not a client story.

Three Kinds of Project, Three Kinds of Pressure

  • Nuclear New Build: Big, Long, Rota-Driven

    A great many workers need a bed near the same few sites. Supply tightens and prices can move fast as a project ramps up. Plan early and tell us your rota.

  • Wind and Grid: Remote Sites, Weekly Rotations

    Crews on the north coast, in the Highlands and along the coast, often on weekly rotations. Suppliers in small places may want seven-night bookings, so odd stay lengths can cost more than you expect.

  • Port-Side Energy: Hubs and Fabrication

    Port and fabrication projects, like those around the Cromarty Firth, bring crews for months. Start dates on big port projects move, so tell us the day yours does.

Housing a crew

What Energy Project Crews Actually Need

  • Rest Is Part of Safety

    Fatigue is a measured cause of incidents, so where a crew sleeps and how long the commute takes belong in the safety conversation. We'll give you honest times, not just distances.

  • Door to Gate

    A short drive can still be a long commute if the site has a car park, a shuttle and a gate. Ask us for realistic door-to-gate times, and tell us if the crew can't use a car.

  • Rotas Decide the Beds

    Ten on, four off? A fortnight on, a week off? The pattern changes the sums. Holding the same house through the days off can cost less than moving people twice, or more. We'll set out both.

  • Large Crews Need Lead Time

    Twenty single rooms is a different job from twenty beds. Give us the numbers as early as you can, and tell us how they might grow.

  • Certificates Before the First Night

    Gas and electrical certificates, insurance and supplier forms are part of the first conversation here, not a scramble in week one. We'll tell you what we can supply and when.

  • A Room Each, with Space to Cook

    Longer stays mean a proper kitchen, somewhere to dry kit and, for night-shift workers, a room that shuts out the day.

Straight answers

What Goes Wrong, and What We Do

Four things we hear again and again, how we handle each, and where we stop.
  1. 01

    The Project Eats the Local Supply

    What we do

    Tell us the programme early and be honest about how it might grow. We'll tell you what we're seeing locally, including when prices are moving, rather than what you'd like to hear.

    What we can't promise

    A project this size moves the market. We can't hold supply that isn't there, and we can't promise today's price in three months.

  2. 02

    The Commute Is Longer Than the Map Says

    What we do

    We ask about the shuttle, the gate and the car park, not just the postcode. Door-to-gate time goes on the shortlist where we can find it.

    What we can't promise

    Some times are estimates until someone has driven them. Tell us what your crews actually find, and we'll use it next time.

  3. 03

    Paperwork Before the First Night

    What we do

    Insurance, certificates and supplier forms are part of the first conversation. The procurement page says what we supply, so your team can read it without a call.

    What we can't promise

    Certificates come from property owners, so some timing is out of our hands. We chase them early and tell you if one is late, because a late certificate is how a start date slips.

  4. 04

    Rotas That Don't Match a Nightly Rate

    What we do

    Give us the rota. We'll set out what it costs to hold a house through the days off against moving people at change-over, so you can choose.

    What we can't promise

    Holding a house can be impossible, and owners may want a minimum stay. Ask us what applies to your project.

Honest fit check

Is Rooost Right for This?

We'd rather tell you now than after a long sourcing job.

A good fit

  • Stays of 4 nights or more, on a set rota for weeks or months.
  • Projects where local supply is thin and prices can move.
  • Buyers who want one contact across a programme.

A hotel is simpler

  • Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call.
  • A short job where a local B&B has rooms and a car park. Use it.

Booking direct

If you've already got owners on the books near site and someone to manage them, that can cost less per night. We can't promise the cheapest rate, and we can't make supply appear where there isn't any. We can tell you early what's realistic.

Questions

Questions Crews Ask Us

Do you house crews working on nuclear projects?
We house crews near big nuclear projects, such as the new builds in Somerset and Suffolk, from mechanical and electrical teams to ground and civils crews. We make no claims about security clearance. There's a straight answer on that further down.
Can you cover a rota like ten days on, four off?
That's the kind of pattern we plan around. Rotas change the sums: holding the same house through the days off can cost less than moving people twice, or more. Give us the rota and we'll set out both options. Ask us what applies to your project.
How do you handle places with hardly any supply?
Honestly. Some of these places have a handful of properties, and a big project takes a large share. We'll tell you early what's near, what's a longer drive and when a price may move. Where we lack enough recent stays for a typical rate, the card says Ask us.
What can you give us for safety and ESG (sustainability) reporting?
Tell us what your project has to report. We'll say honestly what we can give you and what we can't yet, rather than promise a report you haven't seen. Distance to site and where your crew sleep are things we can put in front of you at brief stage.
Can you house crews on security-cleared or defence sites?
We house crews near sites. We don't vet workers, and we don't claim our properties are cleared or approved for secure or defence sites. If your project limits what you can share, tell us what you can, such as the area and dates. Check your client's accommodation rules first.
