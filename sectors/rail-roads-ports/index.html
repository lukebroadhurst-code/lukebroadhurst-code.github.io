Tell us your version

Three Kinds of Week, One Account

  • Rail: a Possession, a Deadline

    A possession (a booked closure of the line) gets confirmed late. If the crew work nights, they sleep when the street is awake, so tell us and we'll look for quiet. Distance to site matters here more than almost anywhere.

  • Roads: a New Town Every Fortnight

    Highways and road-marking crews move on every week or two, with long vans and sometimes a lorry. Each move is a new booking unless somebody plans the run. That's what one account is for.

  • Ports: Several Sites, Short Notice

    Marine and port programmes run across several sites, often at short notice. One account across the lot is tidier than a stack of separate bookings.

Housing a crew

What Rail, Road and Port Crews Actually Need

  • Rest Is a Safety Issue

    Tired people near live traffic is the risk. Distance to site, a quiet room for day sleepers and a proper bed all count. We'll tell you how far each option really is, door to site.

  • Plan the Run

    Planning the whole run up front means fewer last-minute bookings to chase. Send us the weeks and the sites, and tell us what's firm.

  • Long Vans and Lorries

    Sprinters, welfare vans and the odd lorry need real parking, not 'on-street, probably'. Give us the vehicle, the length and the height. Owners' descriptions can be wrong for a big vehicle.

  • Many Sites, One Account

    Ports and marine work often means a booking at each of several sites. One account keeps that tidy.

  • Short Notice Is Normal

    Possessions, mobilisations and weekend closures get confirmed late. We'd rather you ring on day one than day five, and we'll tell you honestly what's possible at short notice.

  • Own Rooms, Bathrooms to Spare

    Crews on this work usually want a room each, and mixed crews need separate bathrooms. Tell us the split and the number of bathrooms you need.

Straight answers

What Goes Wrong, and What We Do

Four things we hear again and again, how we handle each, and where we stop.
  1. 01

    A New Booking Every Week in a New Town

    What we do

    Send us the run: the sites, the weeks and how many. We plan it as one programme, with one point of contact, instead of a fresh booking and a fresh set of emails each time.

    What we can't promise

    Programmes change. A run planned in advance still moves when the work does, so tell us the day it changes.

  2. 02

    Supplier Approval Takes Months, Crews Need Beds This Month

    What we do

    Start the paperwork and a first small project at the same time. Onboarding a host direct takes just as long, and good properties get taken while forms go round. What we supply is on the procurement page.

    What we can't promise

    Large buyers can take weeks or months to approve a new supplier, and we can't speed up your procurement team. We can make our side easy to answer, and start with one project while approval goes through.

  3. 03

    Tired Crews, Long Drives

    What we do

    Distance to site goes on the brief with the shift pattern. We'll tell you how far each option really is, and flag the ones that make for a long day.

    What we can't promise

    A cheaper house further out is sometimes the one option available. If that's the case, we'll say so, and you decide.

  4. 04

    A New Booking, a New Email, Every Time

    What we do

    One account means one place to ask and one person who already knows your sites. You're not explaining the programme again each time.

    What we can't promise

    People go on leave. Ask us who covers when your contact is away.

Is Rooost Right for This?

We'd rather tell you now than after a long sourcing job.

A good fit

  • Stays of 4 nights or more suit us best.

A hotel is simpler

Questions

Questions Crews Ask Us

Do you work around night shifts?
If you tell us, yes. Night crews sleep in the day, so quiet matters. Put it on the brief and we'll look for properties away from busy roads and noisy neighbours. We can't promise silence, but we can ask the right questions before you reserve.
Can you house a crew that moves every week?
That's a normal brief for highways and road marking. Send us the run, the sites and the dates, and we'll plan it as one programme. Dates move, so tell us what's firm. Where a place is a job-by-job quote, we'll say so early.
Our company already has a travel desk and a supplier list. Does that matter?
'It matters, so raise it early. We can sit alongside your travel desk for stays it doesn't suit, like houses for crews over a few weeks. If you need an approved list first, see the procurement page and start the paperwork with a first project.
What about long vans, welfare vans and lorries?
Give us the vehicle type, the length and height, and how many. Parking is part of the brief. Owners' descriptions of parking can be wrong for large vehicles, so more detail from you means a better shortlist.
Do you cover ports and marine work?
We house crews for port and marine projects, usually across several sites. Some ports are a job-by-job quote because we lack enough recent stays for a typical rate. Tell us the port and dates, and we'll say what's realistic.
