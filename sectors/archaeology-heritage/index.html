Housing a crew

What a Dig Team Actually Needs

  • An End Date That's a Forecast

    Weather, finds and the client's programme all move a dig. Tell us the finish is soft, and it changes which houses we show you.

  • Fields and Villages

    Digs are wherever the archaeology is. The nearest beds can be a long drive away, and village supply is thin. We'll be straight about drive times, and about when a longer drive is all there is.

  • Two This Week, Eight Next

    Teams swell for a big trench and shrink for a watching brief (one archaeologist on site while others dig). Tell us the range, not just the number, and we'll look at houses that cope with both ends.

  • Mud, Wet Kit and a Hall That Copes

    Boots, waterproofs and finds bags come in with the team. Tell us, and we'll look for houses with somewhere to dry kit and a hall or utility room that can take mud.

  • Self-Catering That Works

    Long days outdoors mean proper cooking: an oven and hob, a fridge big enough for packed lunches, a washing machine. Say what matters and it goes on the brief.

  • A Budget Set at Tender

    Archaeology budgets are set long before anyone chooses a house. That's a reality we work with, not a problem. Tell us your number and we'll tell you what it buys.

Straight answers

What Goes Wrong, and What We Do

Four things we hear again and again, how we handle each, and where we stop.
  1. 01

    The Weather Moves the Finish

    What we do

    We plan the stay with the finish date as a range. Where the owner can run on, we extend. Where they can't, we look for somewhere close that can, so the team moves once, together, if at all.

    What we can't promise

    Not every owner can extend. We'll tell you which houses can and which can't before you reserve, so a move isn't a surprise.

  2. 02

    The Tender Budget Didn't Price the Area

    What we do

    Ask us for typical rates while you're pricing the bid. We'll tell you what the area costs in the months you'll be there, because holiday counties and coastal towns cost more in summer.

    What we can't promise

    Typical rates are a guide, not a quote. Where we don't have enough recent stays for a place, we say so and quote job by job.

  3. 03

    Heating, Hot Water or Wi-Fi Fails on a Cold Week

    What we do

    Tell us the months you'll be there and we'll ask about heating and hot water before you reserve. If something fails, ring us. We chase the owner, and if it can't be fixed quickly we look at moving the team.

    What we can't promise

    Some repairs depend on a landlord or a plumber, and they have taken longer than we'd like. We'll tell you what's happening, even when the answer isn't a good one.

  4. 04

    One-Night Stays

    What we do

    A single night is a hotel job. For 4 nights or more, a house usually works. Tell us the range of team sizes and the dates, and we'll say what fits.

    What we can't promise

    Suppliers rarely take one-night house bookings. We'd rather tell you that than promise it.

Honest fit check

Is Rooost Right for This?

We'd rather tell you now than after a long sourcing job.

A good fit

  • Stays of 4 nights or more, for a week or longer.
  • Digs, surveys and heritage work that run for weeks across several counties.
  • A tender or bid that needs a realistic accommodation line.

A hotel is simpler

  • Anything under 4 nights.
  • A single site visit that happens to run late. Use a hotel.

Booking direct

A local B&B or a cottage you've used for years can suit a short job. Keep it. We're here for the weeks when the team grows, the dates move and nobody wants to be the one ringing round.

Questions

Questions Crews Ask Us

What happens when the weather delays the dig?
Tell us at the start that the finish is a forecast. If the owner can run on, we extend. If they can't, we look for somewhere nearby that can, and move the team together. We'll say which as early as we can. Ask us what applies to your project.
Can you cope with a team that changes size every week?
That's a common brief. Give us the range, such as two to eight, and we'll look at houses that work at both ends. Say when a number is a guess. Changes are easier early, and good properties get re-let while a decision is pending.
How do I price accommodation into a tender?
Use the typical rates on this page and ask us about your area. In Louth and Alford, a shared house has been £65 per person per night, excluding VAT (based on 46 invoice lines in the last year). It's a guide, not a quote, and the season matters.
Can you work with our budget?
Start from your number and we'll tell you what it buys. Budgets in this sector are set at tender, and that's a reality we work with, not a problem. Where an area can't deliver at that price, you'll hear it from us early, with the nearest alternative.
What do you need from us to start?
The site postcode, rough dates, the team size range, and anything the team can't do without: a kitchen, parking, a room each. Say if the dates might move. That's enough for us to start, and we'll ask for anything else.
