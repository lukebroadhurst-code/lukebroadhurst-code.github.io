Why Rooost

Your Workers Deserve Better

Where people sleep, eat and wind down after a shift has a direct bearing on safety, delivery and whether they come back for the next job. Here is how we think about it, and what we will and will not promise.

The 8-8-8 rule

The 8-8-8 Rule, in Plain English

A day has 24 hours. Eight go on the job and eight on sleep. The middle eight belong to the person.

  • 8

    Work

    On site, delivering the job.

  • 8

    Yours

    To cook, rest and ring home.

  • 8

    Sleep

    In a proper bed, somewhere quiet.

Eight hours work, eight for yourself, eight asleep.

The standard we design stays around, after Robert Owen, 1817.

Everything that eats into the middle eight comes out of them: a long drive, a room with no kitchen, a booking that falls through at 7pm.

A property covers the sleep. The rest of what we do is for the hours in between.

We cannot change the eight on site. We can change where the other sixteen are spent.

People first

People First, Without the Slogans

Most companies say they care about their workforce. Here are three places it shows up, or does not: sleep, distance and kitchens. Each has a number we can source and a limit we will own up to.

01Sleep

Sleep Comes First

Nobody does a long shift well on broken sleep. A proper bed somewhere quiet is not a perk. It is the first of the eight.

That is why we ask about rooms at the start of a brief, not at the end.

What we do about it

  • Ask about rooms on the brief: own room or sharing, and bathrooms per head.
  • Show bedrooms and bathrooms side by side in the shortlist, so nobody is surprised on arrival.
  • Say plainly when a lower rate means sharing, or fewer extras.

What we can't do

  • Promise a quiet street or a mattress everyone loves.
  • Promise a repair time that depends on someone else's plumber or landlord. We tell you what we can do, and how long we think it will take.

02Distance

Distance Comes Out of the Middle

Say a cheaper house is forty minutes from site. That is eighty minutes a day in a van, there and back, often in the dark.

That time is on no accommodation invoice. It comes out of the person.

What we do about it

  • Show drive time to site on every option, and say when it is an off-peak estimate.
  • Weigh distance to site against distance to a shop and a pub. The nearest place is not automatically the right one.
  • Say early when a place is thin on supply, so you can plan around it.

What we can't do

  • Find a place next to every site. In rural areas and on islands the choice is thin, and some places on our map say "Ask us".
  • Make a long drive short. If every option is far out, we tell you, and you decide.

03Kitchens and rooms

A Kitchen Is Not a Luxury

Somebody's partner cooked a meal at the weekend. The least a crew should have in the week is a fridge, a hob and a microwave to reheat it.

A room each. A sofa that is not rock hard. Somewhere to sit with the rest of the crew. That is not luxury. It is treating people properly.

What we look for

  • Kitchen
  • Living space
  • Real bedrooms
  • Wi-Fi
  • Laundry
  • Parking

What we do about it

  • Ask us for the property standard we source against.
  • Tell you what is in the property before you commit. A lower rate can mean fewer extras, and we would rather you knew.
  • Give you a shortlist, not a single take-it-or-leave-it option, so your people have a say.

What we can't do

  • Promise every kettle and pan will be in the cupboard. Things go missing. Tell us and we will sort it.
  • Make a rented house feel like your own home. We aim for the basics, and tell you honestly what you are getting.

Living Away From Home Is Lonely Enough

A shared kitchen and a proper sofa will not fix loneliness. They do give a crew somewhere to be together.

What changes

What Changes When Accommodation's Done Right

Nothing dramatic. The good version is quiet: nobody rings, nobody chases, nobody is up at 9pm sorting a bed.

  • Your crew

    Before

    A budget room a long drive from site. A takeaway for tea. Boots in the back of the van.

    After

    A house near site. A kitchen. A room each where the brief allows.

  • The person who books

    Before

    Texts at 9pm. A spreadsheet and a card statement that disagree.

    After

    A named account manager who picks up the phone. A VAT invoice in your company's name for every stay.

  • The project manager

    Before

    Booking beds on top of the programme.

    After

    A shortlist to choose from, and the programme to run.

  • Finance

    Before

    Receipts from cards and listing sites, and a VAT question nobody can answer.

    After

    One invoice to match to a PO, with VAT shown.

Honest limits

What We Will and Won't Do

Promises you can hold us to are short. Here they are, with the limits we would rather say out loud.

We Will

  • Say what we can and cannot do before you commit.
  • Tell you when a hotel is the better call, even though we do not sell hotel stays.
  • Show you where a place is, how far it is from site and what we know about it, before you say yes.
  • Own it when we get it wrong, in plain words, and fix it.
  • Put our promises in writing, so you can hold us to them.
  • Place your people in properties we would be comfortable recommending.

We Won't

  • Promise the cheapest nightly rate. Going direct is sometimes lower. We can promise to show you the whole bill.
  • Promise a repair time that depends on someone else's plumber.
  • Over-promise. If we cannot deliver something, you hear it from us first.
  • Pretend every place is easy. Islands and remote sites are slower and thinner, and we say so early.
  • Hide how we are paid. One all-in nightly rate. No booking fee on top. We're paid by the properties we place you in.
  • Blame you, or our suppliers, when something goes wrong.

When it goes wrong

When We Get It Wrong

Sometimes we will. When we do, you will hear something like: "We messed up. That's on us." Then four things, in this order.
  1. 01

    Own it

    It was us, and you hear that in the first sentence.

  2. 02

    Explain it

    What happened, briefly, without excuses.

  3. 03

    Fix it

    A specific fix, not a generic apology.

  4. 04

    Follow up

    A real person checks your team is settled. Not a survey.

We do not blame you, and we do not blame our suppliers. If we fall short, our complaints policy says what happens next.

Someone who picks up

Someone Who Knows Your Projects

Most of what goes wrong is not dramatic. A door code. A boiler. A date that moves. What matters then is who you ring.

Our answer is a named account manager who picks up the phone. Someone who knows your projects, so you are not explaining them from scratch every time.

We hold the relationship with the property owner, so you are not chasing a landlord in the evening.
