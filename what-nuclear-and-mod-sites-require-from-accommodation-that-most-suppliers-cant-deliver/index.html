What Nuclear and MOD Sites Need from Accommodation

Vetting, NDAs and data handling change how a cleared programme is housed. What the brief needs before sourcing starts, and what to ask any supplier.

Hinkley Point C has 14,000 workers on site. Sizewell C reached 2,000 in early 2026 and is projected to peak at 17,000. UK civil nuclear employment hit a record of just under 100,000 roles in 2025, with 40,000 additional workers needed across the sector by 2030. The accommodation requirement that sits behind these numbers is not the same brief as a standard infrastructure project. Most contractors find that out after the mobilisation has started.

Key highlights

  • Workers on nuclear and MOD sites require Baseline Personnel Security Standard vetting as a minimum before unrestricted site access.
  • BPSS checks take 7 to 10 working days per worker, which affects how a mobilisation can be sequenced.
  • Accommodation suppliers on cleared programmes often need to sign NDAs before the brief can be shared.
  • Standard booking platforms generate guest records that may not meet programme data handling requirements.

The vetting requirement changes the sequencing

Workers accessing nuclear licensed sites, MOD establishments, and other restricted access infrastructure require Baseline Personnel Security Standard checks as a minimum, covering identity, right to work, criminal record, and a signed declaration, with each check taking 7 to 10 working days per person. On a standard construction mobilisation, accommodation can be sourced and confirmed while the workforce is being assembled. On a cleared programme, the workforce list often cannot be confirmed until the checks have cleared, which means the accommodation supplier is waiting on a headcount that cannot be shared until the programme security requirements allow it.

Accommodation can still be pre-positioned on cleared programmes, provided the supplier understands the sequence well enough to move with it. Holding capacity across a flexible window while the workforce list clears takes a different kind of relationship with property owners than a standard fixed-date booking. Suppliers who have only worked on open programmes book what they can see and release it when the check-in date changes.

What NDAs change about the brief

Accommodation suppliers on classified or commercially sensitive programmes are often required to sign non-disclosure agreements before they can be told what they’re quoting for. This applies equally to the fit-out contractor, the civils team, and the accommodation provider.

The practical effect is that the pre-qualification stage takes longer. The supplier needs to understand what they’re being asked to commit to before any sourcing begins. Programme names, site locations, and headcount figures that would appear on a standard accommodation brief require NDA coverage before they can be shared. For a supplier working this kind of programme for the first time, the process raises questions they haven’t encountered before: what data can they store, how it should be held, how long they can retain it, and who in their organisation is permitted to see it.

The data handling requirements that standard platforms fail

Booking accommodation for a security-cleared workforce through a consumer platform creates records that the programme may not be able to allow. Booking platforms retain guest data across their systems, generate confirmations to email addresses outside the programme’s control, and produce invoices that reference individual names and dates in formats that may not meet the data handling requirements of the programme security plan.

The invoicing requirement is specific. On a cleared programme, invoices often need to reference cost centre codes rather than project names, omit personal data beyond what finance requires for reconciliation, and route through procurement systems that have been included in the programme security arrangements. An invoice from a consumer platform, addressed to a named individual at a site address that appears in the programme’s sensitive documentation, fails those requirements before anyone reads it.

What the brief needs before sourcing begins

The accommodation brief for a nuclear or MOD programme needs to answer 4 questions before any property search starts. What is the security classification of the programme, and what does that mean for who the supplier can brief and what they can record? What is the vetting timeline, and how does it affect the headcount confirmation date? What are the data handling requirements for guest records, invoices, and communications? And has the supplier signed or been offered the relevant NDA?

Because the answers to those questions shape every subsequent sourcing decision, the contractors who ask them early are the ones who avoid spending the first 2 weeks of a cleared programme unwinding accommodation arrangements that fail the security plan while the workforce waits.

  • UK civil nuclear employment record ~100,000 roles, 40,000 additional workers needed by 2030:Rullion, 2026

What this means for you

  • "Contractor accommodation" is not one problem. A rail night shift, an offshore rotation and a six-week dig need different briefs.
  • Start with the site, the start date and how many of you. The sector decides the rest of the questions.
  • If your sector is not here, ask us. The questions stay the same: distance, length of stay and notice.
