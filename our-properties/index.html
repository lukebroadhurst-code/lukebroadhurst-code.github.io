Three Kinds of Property

These aren't listings. Each card illustrates a kind of property crews have stayed in, in an area where we place crews. The rate is a real one, from our invoices. The building isn't named.

How to read a card

The rate
Typical per person, per night, excluding VAT. Taken from what we actually invoiced in the last year. A guide, not a quote: dates, stay length and the property decide the final rate.
What it covers
Cleaning and parking are in the rate in almost every booking, and if something is extra we tell you before you reserve.
How long
Stays of 4 nights or more suit us best. Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call.
If a card doesn't say
Bedroom and bathroom counts aren't recorded stay by stay, so a card describes the kind of place, not a count. Where a card doesn't say, tell us what matters to your crew and we'll find out.

What we ask for

What We Ask for in a Property

This is what we ask for when we build a shortlist. We don't tick anything as checked until our team has confirmed it for a property, and where we don't have an answer yet, we say so. They are the questions any crew should get answered before the keys change hands.

  • A room each, or a straight answer

    Sleep is the reason for the property. Some crews are happy to share, some can't, and nobody should find out at the door.

    Tell us who needs their own room. It changes which houses are right.

  • Bathrooms for the headcount

    A full crew and one shower makes for a bad start. Bathrooms per head decides whether the mornings work.

    Ask us how many bathrooms there would be for your headcount.

  • Van parking that fits the van

    The van carries the tools, and the tools are the job. A host's "parking for two" can mean two small cars.

    Tell us what you drive, and how many. "Parking" can mean very different things.

  • A kitchen you can cook in

    No kettle, one pot, no plates: it happens, so it's on the list. A crew that cooks needs more than a hob.

    Ask us what's in the kitchen before you commit.

  • Washing and drying

    Boots, hi-vis and wet kit come home every day, and need to be dry by morning.

    Wet kit most days? Tell us, and ask us where it dries.

  • Heat, hot water and Wi-Fi that work

    These are the faults crews notice first, and a landlord can take a while to fix them.

    Ask us how heating, hot water and Wi-Fi are confirmed before you arrive.

  • Gas, electrical and insurance paperwork

    Your procurement team will ask for the certificates. It's easier to have them before the crew arrives than after something goes wrong.

    Need gas, electrical and insurance paperwork for your own compliance? Say so, and ask us what we can supply.

  • A drive you can live with

    A short drive beats a cheap bed. Crews also want a shop and a pub within reach, not an industrial estate.

    Give us your site postcode, and ask us for the drive and for how far the shops are.

  • Quiet enough to sleep

    Early starts and night shifts both mean sleeping at odd hours. Noisy neighbours and the wrong house rules wreck it.

    Early starts or night shifts? Tell us, and ask us about noise.

  • A host who says yes to crews

    Some hosts turn subcontractors away, sometimes after the crew has arrived. We'd rather know first.

    Some hosts turn crews and subcontractors away. Ask us how we handle it.

Tell us what matters most to your crew, and we'll start there.

When it goes wrong

What Happens When a Property Isn't Right

It will happen to someone. We'd rather tell you how it works now than have you find out in week three.

  1. 01

    Tell us

    Phone or email, as soon as you spot it. A photo or video helps, because hosts often ask for one before they send anyone.

    We're on the phone Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 18:00, on 01926 351855. Outside office hours, the same number goes to an out-of-hours line that a member of the Rooost team answers.

  2. 02

    We chase it, or move you

    We chase the host. If it can't be fixed, we'll tell you straight and work out the next move with you.

  3. 03

    Straight about the timing

    How long it takes depends on who owns the fix.

Ours to fix

Some fixes are ours to make, like finding your team another property.

Not ours to fix

When it waits on a landlord, plumber or managing agent, it can take days.

Who pays for what depends on what went wrong and on your terms. Ask us before you book, not after.

Straight answers

Questions About Our Properties

Why can't I see the actual properties?
Because they aren't ours to publish. Hosts trust us with their homes, and a public list of addresses would end that trust. So each card shows a typical kind of place, the area and the rate. The specifics come with your shortlist.
Are the typical stays real?
They're kinds of property crews have stayed in, in the areas shown, and the rates come from what we invoiced in the last year. The pictures show the type of place, not a specific building. A card isn't a booking, and you can't book one by name.
What if a card doesn't say something I need?
That means it isn't on record for that kind of stay, so we don't guess. Where a card doesn't say, tell us what matters to your crew and we'll find out.
What does the rate cover?
Typical rates are per person, per night, excluding VAT. Cleaning and parking are in the rate in almost every booking, and if something is extra we tell you before you reserve.
Do you inspect every property?
Ask us how a property gets onto a shortlist, and what we ask the host for.
Can I choose where my crew stays?
You get a shortlist, not a single option. Tell us your site postcode, how many of you there are and what you drive, and we narrow it down from there.
What if the van doesn't fit, or there's nowhere to dry wet kit?
Tell us before you commit, not after. Van size and drying space are both on our list, and a property that fails either is the wrong property for your crew.
Do you book hotels?
Stays of 4 nights or more suit us best. Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call. We do not sell hotel stays. We use a hotel only in an emergency, or when dates do not line up (for example, one night while a property is booked out).
