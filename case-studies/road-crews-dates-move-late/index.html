Rolling Road Crews and Dates That Move Late

An office manager booked accommodation for road crews working nights, with dates that firmed up late. She put a request in, cancelled it within minutes and put it in again. This is how the first bookings went.

01The situation

Where It Started

A road-works contractor sends crews to a new stretch of road, often at short notice. The office manager books their accommodation, and the dates are rarely settled early. They firm up when the contracts team confirms the work.

The crews work nights. A supervisor checks in at the start of the week, the team works through the week, sleeps most of the last day and checks out that evening.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

The first request was wrong within minutes. The office manager sent it, then wrote that she had cancelled it because the contracts team had just changed the dates. She put it through again with the new dates.

Later it was our turn. A place we had lined up for one enquiry was booked by someone else before the supplier blocked the dates, and her team ended up putting that crew in a hotel.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    We took each request as it came and changed it when the dates changed. We sent the invoice the same afternoon as her first request, and it was paid the day the crew arrived.

  2. 02

    Within weeks she was putting several enquiries through in a morning and asking whether we had found anything. We went round the suppliers for each one and told her what was and was not available.

04Owning it

What Went Wrong, and How We Fixed It

Things go wrong with any accommodation. What matters is who tells you, and how fast it is sorted.

A Crew Picked Up a Parking Ticket

What happened
A crew did not read their check-in message properly and picked up a parking ticket.
Our part in it
The details were in the message, but a message that gets missed has not done its job. A crew should not pay for that.
What we did
We sent both vehicle registrations to the property for a parking permit and offered to reimburse the ticket, four minutes after she asked.
How it ended
She wrote back that it was very much appreciated, and that the crew would photograph the ticket and send it over.

A Property Went to Another Booking

What happened
A place we had lined up for one of her enquiries was booked by someone else before the supplier blocked the dates.
Our part in it
We source from property partners, so their slip lands on us. She should hear it from us, with a way forward.
What we did
We told her the moment we knew, said we were going round our suppliers for something else, and asked how far her team could stretch.
How it ended
Her team decided to put that crew in a hotel and told us not to worry about it. Her other enquiry for the same week went ahead.

05The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

06The result

What It Meant

The first request was cancelled minutes after it was sent and put through again the same day, the first invoice was paid on the day the crew arrived, and within weeks the office manager was sending several enquiries at once.

Not every enquiry landed. One went to a hotel, and we would rather say so.

What This Means If You're in Rail, Roads and Ports

  • If your crews move on a week's notice, tell us how the work really runs. "Next week, maybe a different week" is a normal brief, as long as we know it might move.
  • If your crews work nights, say so. We need to know when they sleep and where the vehicles stay, because it changes which places fit.
  • Put the parking rules where your supervisor will see them first. In this story they were in the check-in message, and a ticket still happened.
