Ground engineering and surveys,  · Several regions

Site Crews, Booked Two Weeks at a Time

A contractor booked every crew through one booking platform, because it was quick and easy. Then it gave us a handful of jobs in a week, booked two weeks at a time. Two of our first invoices were wrong, and we say so.

A UK ground engineering contractor
Sector
Ground engineering and surveys

Figures counted

How it is shown

01The situation

Where It Started

The person who books a contractor's crews wrote to us early on that they book a lot, but everything goes through one booking platform because it is quick and easy.

Then they told us about a weekend. A crew had checked out and left something behind. The door code had changed, the host could not be reached, and someone spent a long time on hold with the platform.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

Booking trip by trip is quick when nothing goes wrong. When something did, one person was left chasing a host at the weekend.

The jobs were awkward to plan too. Most ran for weeks or months, but the contractor booked them two weeks at a time.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    The first list came the day we met: a handful of jobs, from one week to four months, with the crew sizes and the postcodes. Some were extensions of jobs already running, and some were new starts.

  2. 02

    We booked in two-week blocks, the way the contractor wanted, and extended each one as the job ran on. When they asked for a lower-priced option for one town we sent two, and they picked.

  3. 03

    We billed per job. When they asked for copies of everything outstanding so each invoice could get a PO number, we sent them and a statement that morning, and copied more of their team in from then on.

04Owning it

What Went Wrong, and How We Fixed It

Things go wrong with any accommodation. What matters is who tells you, and how fast it is sorted.

Two Invoices Were Wrong

What happened
Soon after, the contractor told us two invoices were wrong. One billed a house beyond the date the job had been extended to. The other billed for more people than were staying.
Our part in it
Both were our errors. An invoice you have to query is not doing its job.
What we did
Our finance team sent a corrected invoice the same day each time, and asked what was wrong when the message just said it was incorrect.
How it ended
Both corrected invoices went out the same day. The next morning they asked for copies of everything outstanding so each could get a PO, and we sent them.

05The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

06The result

What It Meant

In the first week the contractor sent us the jobs on its list. Within six months we had raised more than 40 invoices for it.

It began with a handful of jobs in a week and grew job by job.

What This Means If You're in Ground Engineering and Surveys

  • If you book jobs two weeks at a time, say so. We would rather extend a booking every fortnight than quote a month you will not use.
  • If you already book through a platform and it works, keep it for what it does well. The question is who rings the host at the weekend when a door code has changed.
  • Tell us which purchase order goes on which job before the first invoice. It saves an invoice coming back.

About This Story

The client is described by sector and region, not by name, and we change details so you cannot tell who it is: places are widened, dates are dropped and volumes are rounded. Every figure shows how we worked it out, and we leave out any we cannot trace.
