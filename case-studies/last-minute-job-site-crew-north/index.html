Ground engineering and surveys,  · The north, then England

A Last-Minute Job for a Site Crew in the North

A colleague at a contractor wrote with a four-week job in the north, starting the following week, at short notice. It was booked in seven days, and by the autumn we had invoiced stays in three regions.

01The situation

Where It Started

A contractor sends crews to sites around the UK, often at short notice, and already had its own way of finding places. For at least one site that meant a larger crew in budget hotels.

After an early conversation they said they liked what they had seen. A week or so later a colleague wrote: a four-week job in the north was starting the following week, up to four people, self-catering.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

The job gave them days, not weeks. The first start date slipped by a week, the team grew by one, and the company that owned the work had not yet confirmed.

And on the day we were due to go through the options, the colleague who knew the job fell ill. Someone else took it over mid-stream.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    We set up a meeting for the next day, and met the team again before the end of the week.

  2. 02

    We sent a shortlist: a house three could share, and apartments for the rest. They asked us to price the house plus apartments and to leave one set of options out. The next day we raised the first invoice, with the job number they sent.

  3. 03

    They had more to give us: a longer site in England for a few people, and another where the crew was in hotels. We started looking at those next.

  4. 04

    We sent a local-offers card for the crew. They wrote that they would love it.

04The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

05The result

What It Meant

The first job was booked in a week. By the autumn we had raised at least 14 invoices, with stays in 3 regions.

It started with a quiet conversation and a job nobody could plan.

What This Means If You're in Ground Engineering and Surveys

  • If a job lands on you with a week to go, send what you have: the postcode, the start day, who is coming, and "maybe one more". A rough brief this week is more use than a perfect one next week.
  • If your plans depend on a client confirming the work, say so early. It explains why the dates move, and we can plan for it.
  • If part of your crew is in hotels today and you have a longer site coming, send us both. We can look at the longer one while the short one is being booked.

About This Story

The client is described by sector and region, not by name, and we change details so you cannot tell who it is: places are widened, dates are dropped and volumes are rounded. Every figure shows how we worked it out, and we leave out any we cannot trace.
