A Rail Crew, Twenty Rooms and a Week to Sort It

Someone at a rail contractor had a week to house a large crew, and the crew list kept changing. We found houses, swapped one at the last minute, and kept up with a plan that changed daily.

  • Anonymised

01The situation

Where It Started

A rail contractor needed a crew in one city for a week. The person who books it told us early on that she would not secure anything yet, because the job could change.

Then the job firmed up. Within days she asked for twenty individual rooms for a week, with a chance it would run longer.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

The crew plan would not stay still. The room list changed again and again over four days. Some of the crew decided they preferred a hotel with meal deals, so she booked those herself, and the number of rooms she needed from us kept shifting.

Small needs piled up as well: a house to suit one crew member's request, a request for a house with three double rooms, and a boiler fault at one house the day before check-in.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    We told her the same day that the places were a mix of two to four bedroom houses and apartments, all with parking. The next day we had 23 rooms confirmed, with other suppliers lined up for the rest.

  2. 02

    We worked from her latest list every time. Rooms came off, a house for a special request went on, and a colour-coded sheet made it easy to see who was where.

  3. 03

    When she asked for a house with three double rooms, we told her that morning that none of our suppliers had one, and offered a four-bed with three doubles instead.

  4. 04

    She asked for an invoice so she could pay. We sent it that afternoon and it was paid the next day. On the last day, five of the crew needed one more night, and we checked and said yes.

04Owning it

What Went Wrong, and How We Fixed It

Things go wrong with any accommodation. What matters is who tells you, and how fast it is sorted.

A Boiler Fault the Day Before Check-In

What happened
The day before check-in, we learned that the boiler at a larger house had a fault.
Our part in it
A house that is not ready is our problem, not yours. Hearing it the day before is late, and it is also the point where we can still do something about it.
What we did
We found another house that morning and sent her the new details with the others.
How it ended
The new house's details reached her at lunchtime on check-in day, a couple of hours after the rest, and we said sorry for the delay.

05The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

06The result

What It Meant

The invoice was paid the day after it was sent, and one night was added on the last day without fuss.

Part of the crew chose a hotel with meal deals and booked it themselves. The houses went to the people who wanted them.

What This Means If You're in Rail, Roads and Ports

  • If your crew list changes daily, send it anyway. A sheet with one line per person, colour-coded, made this one easy to follow, and we worked from the latest version each time.
  • If some of your crew would rather have a hotel, book those yourselves and tell us who is left. We would rather house the right number than guess.
  • Tell us about special needs early: a ground-floor bedroom, a double room, a room near the door. They decide which houses we can offer, and some, like three double rooms in one house, are hard to find.
