Moving a Project Crew From Hotels to Houses

A project manager on a large energy project had a crew used to hotels and a director who wanted to see the paperwork first. The brief changed twice before anything was booked, and we have invoiced the stay every month since the summer.

01The situation

Where It Started

A contractor on a large energy project had a crew of around ten on a rotation of ten days on and four off, for a few months. Until then the project manager's teams had stayed in budget hotels. He told us he would prefer a house this time.

He could not sign it off alone. A director wanted to see the insurances and the gas and electrical certificates for the properties first, and we were not yet an approved supplier.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

The brief kept shrinking. The crew on the rotation was cut to a handful to begin with, and work on part of the site was put on hold, so three properties became two. Every change reset the quote.

Availability moved too. A large house we had lined up was booked by someone else, and the other properties could be held for a day or so while the approvals went through.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    We sent options with photos and a written breakdown, with a recommendation, and set up his enquiries once he had reset his account password.

  2. 02

    We answered the director's questions in writing, on insurance and on the gas and electrical certificates, and sent our standard terms with a note that any change could be agreed in writing. A draft agreement had been asked for, so that is how we framed it.

  3. 03

    We re-quoted each time the crew size changed, and we told him plainly that properties in the area change hourly, so a decision sooner was better than a decision later.

04Owning it

What Went Wrong, and How We Fixed It

Things go wrong with any accommodation. What matters is who tells you, and how fast it is sorted.

A Property Went to Another Booking

What happened
While the director's approval was pending, a large house we had lined up was booked by someone else.
Our part in it
We source from property partners, so a house going to someone else while we wait is ours to manage, not his.
What we did
We asked the other partners to hold their properties for a day, told the project manager that morning, and re-quoted around what was left.
How it ended
He confirmed on a revised plan, and we have invoiced the stay every month since the summer.

05The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

06The result

What It Meant

It took about three weeks from his first email to a confirmed booking. A lot of that was sign-off inside his company, and we planned around it.

Since the summer we have invoiced the stay every month: at least 4 invoices at the last count.

What This Means If You're in Nuclear, Energy and Renewables

  • If your director wants to see insurance and gas and electrical certificates before anything is signed, ask for the list on day one. On a first project it is often the longest wait.
  • If the crew size is not settled, give us the range and the rotation, for example ten days on and four off. We can plan around a range. A single number that later changes costs more time.
  • Hotels are fine for a week or a short job. For a rotation of ten days on and four off over months, a house the crew can leave their things in is worth asking about.
