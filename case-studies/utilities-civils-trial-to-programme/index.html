From One Trial Week to Hundreds of Invoices

Someone in the office at a utilities contractor was booking squads on company cards, often at the last minute. We started with one apartment for two people. Within months the invoices were in their hundreds.

01The situation

Where It Started

A utilities and civils contractor moves squads of operatives around the UK, and one person in the office books much of it. She was booking on company cards through a booking platform, often at the last minute, and waiting on hosts for check-in details until the last hour.

The plan was to trial a couple of bookings first. Her first request to us was for two operatives who had just joined the company, for three nights.

02The problem

What Was Breaking

The first week showed the gaps. The new starters had no company phones, so she asked us to send the check-in details to her instead. She offered to pay by card, so we sent an invoice.

The other squads that week went elsewhere. And on the last day she passed on that the two operatives had found no wifi.

03The fix

What We Did

  1. 01

    A member of our operations team welcomed the guests in person on arrival and checked in again later in the week. We asked for their van registration so it could be added to the parking permits before they arrived.

  2. 02

    She gave us a reference for the first booking, so her accounts team could match the invoice to the job. We kept going: each time a booking suited her, it came through us.

  3. 03

    The ledger tells the rest. From that first apartment, the invoices kept growing until we had raised more than 400 in under a year.

04Owning it

What Went Wrong, and How We Fixed It

Things go wrong with any accommodation. What matters is who tells you, and how fast it is sorted.

The Wifi Did Not Work and We Heard Late

What happened
The two operatives in the first booking had no wifi, and we did not hear until their last day.
Our part in it
A property without working wifi is our fault, whoever finds out first. Hearing it on the last day is a gap in how we check in.
What we did
We told her it was a shame, because we could have sorted it straight away if we had known.

05The receipt

The Numbers

Each figure sits on a receipt line, with how we worked it out underneath.

How to Read This Receipt

  • At least means we counted what we could link in our own records. The true figure may be higher, never lower.
  • A time or a count is one event or one tally. It is not a promise about the next one.
  • There is no price on it. Our rates are on the place cards further down, per person, per night.

06The result

What It Meant

In her first week the other squads went elsewhere. Since then we have raised more than 400 invoices for her.

It began with two people, three nights and one wifi fault.

What This Means If You're in Construction and Civil Engineering

  • If one person books for squads across the country, give each booking a reference. It makes every invoice easy to match to a job.
  • If your new starters have no company phone yet, tell us who gets the check-in details. We can send them to you, as we did here.
  • Tell us the same day if something is wrong. A wifi fault found on the last day was a wifi fault we could have fixed on the first.
