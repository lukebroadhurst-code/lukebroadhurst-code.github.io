For teams who already have a way

Switching to Rooost

You already have a way of putting crews up. It probably works some of the time. This page is an honest look at each way people do it: where it holds up, where it breaks, and what would change.

The headline change

Many Hands, One Point of Contact

Switching is not about replacing your team. It is about taking the sourcing, the chasing and the calls off them, so the people you have get back to the day job.

Before

Everyone Does a Bit of It

  • A booker, a project manager and a finance contact, each doing part of it around their day jobs.
  • A spreadsheet, a card statement and a folder of receipts that do not quite agree.
  • Several hosts and suppliers, each with their own rules.
  • Evening and weekend calls land on whoever's phone is nearest.
  • A gap whenever someone is off sick or on leave.

After

Same Team, Backed by Rooost

  • A named account manager who picks up the phone is your point of contact for day-to-day requests.
  • Your team stays in charge of the decisions. We take the sourcing, the chasing and the queries.
  • A VAT invoice in your company's name for every stay.
  • Outside office hours, the same number goes to an out-of-hours line that a member of the Rooost team answers.: so the call goes to us, not to whoever booked.
  • Their time goes back to the day job.

Five Ways People Do It, Honestly

We name no names here, just the habits. Each one has a reason, and some are the right answer for some jobs. Find the one that sounds like you.
  • 1. Booking It Yourself on Listing Sites
  • 2. Hotels and Corporate Accounts
  • 3. A Spreadsheet, a Company Card and One Busy Person
  • 4. Going Direct with Landlords and Agents
  • 5. Another Accommodation Specialist

Way 1 of 5

Booking It Yourself on Listing Sites

Why people do it. It is fast and familiar. The filters are good, you can cancel late, and the headline rate is often the lowest on the page.

Where it works fine

A one-off crew, short notice, nobody with time to talk to anyone. If your host accepts subcontractors and you already get a VAT invoice you can use, it can be a perfectly sensible way to book.

Where it breaks for crews

  • Hosts who say no to subcontractors, sometimes after you have booked.
  • Door codes that arrive at 8pm, or not at all.
  • No VAT invoice in your company's name, and card receipts to reconcile at month end.
  • Whoever booked becomes the helpline when something goes wrong.
  • Extra cleaning charges, and hosts who message your crew to go direct.

What changes with us

  • We hold the relationship with the property owner. Changes, extensions and moves come to us.
  • Ask us how check-in details reach your crew.
  • A VAT invoice in your company's name for every stay.
  • One call, one invoice, one point of contact.
  • It will not be cheaper per night every time. Sometimes the listing is. We would rather you compared the whole bill.

Way 2 of 5

Hotels and Corporate Accounts

Why people do it. A credit account, easy cancellation, parking, a price you can predict, and often a travel policy that says to use them.

Where it works fine

Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call. That is what hotels are built for. Use them, and we will tell you if a hotel is the better answer for your brief. We do not sell hotel stays ourselves.

Where it breaks for crews

  • Bigger crews mean more rooms, and the bill climbs with every one.
  • Rates jump when the town is busy, and your job does not move.
  • No kitchen, so everyone eats out, night after night, for weeks.
  • Crews who will not share turn every job into single rooms.
  • Breakfast opens after the van has left.

What changes with us

  • A house or apartment instead: a kitchen, parking for the vans, and a bed each where the brief allows.
  • You see drive time, parking, bedrooms and bathrooms side by side before you decide.
  • A VAT invoice in your company's name for every stay.
  • Keep your hotel account for what it does well. We sit beside it, not instead of it.

Way 3 of 5

A Spreadsheet, a Company Card and One Busy Person

Why people do it. It has worked so far. Accommodation is a side job, project managers are trusted to buy, and nobody senior sees the total.

Where it works fine

Small, steady, one site, and a booker who is good at it and rarely under pressure. If that is you, you may not need us. We would rather say so than take your time.

Where it breaks for crews

  • Everything depends on one person. When they are off, so is the system.
  • "Where am I staying tonight?" arrives by text at 9pm.
  • The spreadsheet and the card statement never quite agree.
  • Nobody owns the total, so nobody can say what it costs.
  • No record of where your people sleep, if a client or a tender asks.

What changes with us

  • Your booker stays in charge of the decision. We carry the workload.
  • A clear record of every booking, change and invoice.
  • One invoice to match to your PO, instead of a pile of receipts.
  • Prefer email and the phone to a portal? That works too.

Way 4 of 5

Going Direct with Landlords and Agents

Why people do it. The nightly rate can be lower. You know the market, a long let feels cheaper, and someone in the business has a landlord they trust.

Where it works fine

On a long, steady project at one site, going direct can beat us on rate, and we have lost work to exactly that. If you have the time to manage leases, deposits, repairs and bills, it can be the right call.

Where it breaks for crews

  • Every property is its own contract, its own bills and its own landlord to chase.
  • Deposits sit with landlords while your programme moves.
  • A new supplier can take weeks to onboard, and the place is let by then.
  • The gas, electrical and insurance paperwork is yours to collect and check.
  • Nobody to call when the landlord does not pick up.

What changes with us

  • One supplier on your approved list, not many to onboard.
  • Ask us what we check before your crew arrives.
  • We hold the relationship with the owner and take the calls.
  • That layer has a cost. The pricing page shows typical rates by place, so you can weigh it for yourself.

Way 5 of 5

Another Accommodation Specialist

Why people do it. They know the work. A good account manager, reliable supply in awkward places, and terms that already suit you.

Where it works fine

If your current specialist is good, stay. A good account manager is worth more than a few pounds a night, and we will not try to talk you out of it.

Where it breaks for crews

  • The person you rely on moves on.
  • A job outgrows the stock one supplier has in one place.
  • You need more places, faster, than they can find.
  • You have no second option when it matters.

What changes with us

  • Start with one project and compare the two side by side.
  • Keep the supplier you trust where they are strong. Use us where you need more, or a second pair of hands.
  • Or move across, once you have seen it work.

Existing suppliers

Keep Them. Seriously.

We are not asking you to drop anyone. Keep your travel desk for hotels. Keep the landlord who answers at seven in the morning. Keep the specialist you trust.

Send us one project and compare the two side by side. If we are better where it matters to you, send us more. If we are not, you have spent one brief finding out.

Your first week

What the First Week Looks Like

Switching is not a project in itself. Here is what happens, in the order it happens. Timings appear where we can stand behind them.

  1. Before you brief

    Tell Us What You Use Today

    What you book with now, who raises bookings, and who gets the invoice. If finance needs a PO number or project code on it, say so now.

  2. Brief

    Where, When, How Many

    A brief is three questions: where is the site, when does it start, how many of you. Add parking, rooms and anything else your crew needs.

  3. Shortlist

    Options to Choose From

    Options back fast. Houses or apartments that fit, with drive time, parking, and bedrooms and bathrooms per head side by side. You choose.

  4. Reserved

    We Hold the Relationship

    We reserve it and deal with the owner. Ask us how check-in details reach your crew.

  5. Reported

    One Invoice, Laid Out Your Way

    Tell us what your accounts team needs on an invoice, and we will say plainly what we can do.

What We Need From You

  • The site postcode
  • The start date, or "not sure yet"
  • How many of you, and how many nights a week
  • Parking, rooms and anything else your crew needs
  • Your PO number or project code, if finance wants one
  • Who gets the invoice

Not sure of something? Say so. We would rather start with what you know.

Fit

Who We Are Not For

Better to say it now than to waste your time. Here is who we help well, and who is better served elsewhere.

A Good Fit

  • Stays of 4 nights or more suit us best.
  • Built for companies running more than one project at once.
  • Sites where hotels are thin on the ground, or crews who would rather cook than eat out.
  • Anyone who would like the booking off a busy desk.

Probably Not Us

  • Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call.
  • Companies where everyone books and claims for themselves and nobody wants that to change. Talk to us when someone wants to own the number.
  • Anyone for whom the lowest possible nightly rate is the whole decision. Going direct can beat us there.
  • Very remote places and islands. Supply is thin and speed suffers, so some places on our map say "Ask us". Ask before you plan around it.

Questions

Before You Switch

What if our current supplier is good?

Keep them. Send us one project and compare the two side by side. A good account manager is worth a lot, and we will not try to talk you out of it.

Do we have to use your portal?

No. Email and phone work. If you would like to see shortlists, bookings and reporting in one place, take a tour of the portal or ask us about it.

Will our crew notice the change?

They will notice the place before they notice us: a house near the site, a kitchen, a room each where the brief allows. If something is not right, they have a number to ring.

What if a property is not right when we arrive?

Tell us straight away. We will fix it or find somewhere else, and say plainly how long that will take. Some fixes depend on an owner or a plumber, and their timing is not ours to promise.

Can we start with just one site?

Yes. One project is the usual way in. Compare us with your current way, then decide whether to send more.
