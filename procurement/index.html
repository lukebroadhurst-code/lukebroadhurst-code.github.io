Purchase orders

From Brief to Paid, in Five Steps

A PO (purchase order) is your formal go-ahead to spend. Here is where it fits, and who does what.
  1. 01

    Brief

    You do this

    Whoever you have approved sends the brief, by form or by phone. Quote your PO or project code from the start, so we can raise it with you early.

  2. 02

    Shortlist

    We do this

    We send a short list that fits your brief. You and the project manager choose.

  3. 03

    Reserved

    Both of us

    You confirm with your PO or a written go-ahead, and we reserve it. How and when you pay is agreed in writing first. Changes, extensions and moves come to us, not to your booker.

  4. 04

    Invoice

    We do this

    Tell us what your accounts team needs on an invoice, and we will say plainly what we can do. Query any line before you pay and we will sort it.

The framework

The Framework, in Seven Parts

A framework is a written agreement for working together across projects. In plain English: what you can expect from us, how we measure it, and what happens if it goes wrong. This is what we put on the table. It is open to discussion.
1. Property standard: set once, applied everywhere

We agree a minimum standard with you, once, and source every enquiry against it. Ask us for the property standard we source against.

You can raise it. We will tell you plainly what that does to the rate.

2. Measures you set

You choose what gets measured. Typical ones: how fast we send options, how quickly we respond when something goes wrong, and what your people say about the stay. We report against them, so the review is about actuals, not our word.

Some things are not ours to time. A landlord's plumber is not ours to schedule, so we do not promise a repair time. We agree what we do control: how fast we respond, and how quickly we offer another place if a fix will take a while.

3. Duty of care

We agree how we record where your people are staying and what we checked before they arrived, and how a problem gets raised and owned until it is fixed. Nobody sorts their own problem.

Outside office hours, the same number goes to an out-of-hours line that a member of the Rooost team answers.

4. ESG and reporting

ESG stands for environmental, social and governance: the reporting sustainability teams and tenders ask for.

We agree with you what data your team needs, and we say plainly what we can supply and what we cannot yet.

5. Cost management

We agree a rate held for the period of the framework, and forward visibility of your projects, so we can source ahead rather than react. Consolidated invoicing if you want it. One all-in nightly rate. No booking fee on top. We're paid by the properties we place you in.

6. Wider team support

Whatever else the business needs from an accommodation partner: evidence and pricing for tender submissions, PQQ (pre-qualification questionnaire) answers, social value responses, and mobilisation planning (getting a team in place when a job starts).

7. Governance and admin

We agree how invoices are laid out and what goes on them. Tell us what your accounts team needs on an invoice, and we will say plainly what we can do.

Procurement Questions, Answered Straight

We already use a travel management company or hotel platform. Do we have to change anything?

Not to start with. Keep it for hotels, flights and short trips. We handle the houses and apartments crews need for longer stays, and one invoice from us sits beside your platform's reporting.
