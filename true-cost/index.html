How the receipt adds up
  • Sticker. People times nights times weeks times rate. The number most people think the stay costs.
  • Platform commission. A share of the sticker that is priced into the rate and never itemised. It is shown for information and is never added to the totals, because it is already inside the sticker.
  • VAT. If your rate includes VAT, it is a sixth of the sticker. If it is ex VAT, it is 20% on top. Either way it only comes back with a valid VAT invoice in your company's name from a VAT-registered supplier.
  • Counting the VAT. The likely total counts it, as the report does. If your rate already includes VAT, the sticker holds it too, so untick the box if your budget already treats VAT as a cost.
  • Programme overrun. The overrun percentage applied to the sticker, as extra weeks at today's rate. Rounded down.
  • Cancellation exposure. Houses times the tail nights times the nightly rate of one house. A maximum if the job ends early on terms that charge a long-stay tail.
  • One leaver. The 2014 cost of replacing one employee, source 34. Not adjusted for inflation. Counted by default, as the report's headline is; untick it to leave it out.
  • Booker hours. Hours a week times weeks. Left in hours because they are real and nobody invoices them.
  • Totals. Likely is sticker plus VAT plus overrun. Possible is likely plus cancellation exposure, plus a leaver if you count one. Fatigue and the audit trail have no figure.

The Rate Itself

Three nightly numbers, and none is the real one.

02

The Commission Inside the Rate

A fee you never see as a line.

Decided by how you buy

Read the detailHide the detail for The Commission Inside the Rate

Listing platforms are paid through a fee, usually charged to the host. Hosts price it into the nightly rate, so you never see it as a line on anything.

None of this is a scandal. It's how platforms are paid, and for a family booking a week in Devon it's a fair trade for choice. For a contractor placing thousands of nights on one project, it adds up before anyone has slept anywhere.

Part of every platform-booked night pays for the platform, not the room. You can only avoid it by booking direct with the property, or through someone who does.

The fee schedules behind this are not named in the report, so the figure is an assumption you can change in the calculator. See source 9 and source 10 for the caveat.

03

The VAT That Doesn't Come Back

Recoverable in principle. How you buy decides.

Decided by how you buy

Read the detailHide the detail for The VAT That Doesn't Come Back

HMRC says a business can reclaim VAT on accommodation its staff need while working away, block bookings included. There are two conditions: the supplier is VAT registered, and you hold a valid VAT invoice addressed to your business. (Source 11, source 12.)

So how the stay is bought decides whether the VAT comes back.

  1. The allowance. The lads get a fixed sum and sort themselves out. Nothing comes back.
  2. A platform or a company card. A platform receipt in a card-holder's name, usually from a host who isn't VAT registered. Usually nothing comes back, and there's no proper invoice for the audit file.
  3. A hotel or a serviced supplier. Invoiced to the company, with a VAT number on the paper. The VAT can come back.

20% standard UK VAT rate on accommodation. This is not tax advice. Check your own VAT position with your accountant.

04

The Hidden Employee

Somebody's whole job, buried inside another job.

Decided by how you buy

Read the detailHide the detail for The Hidden Employee

Someone in your office is running a second job. Every contractor with a crew away has a part-time travel agent: an office manager, a project manager, sometimes a director's PA, occasionally the director. They're good at it. It was never what they were hired for.

Count the transactions. Five houses booked in 28-night blocks is a booking per house every four weeks, before the extensions, the swap when two people can't share, the rebook when a host cancels and the "where am I staying tonight" texts at 9pm. Then finance gets the receipts.

Finance sees the receipts, not the hours. That's why the calculator counts this line in hours: they're real, and nobody invoices them.

05

The Job Slips. The Booking Doesn't.

Overruns and early finishes both cost money.

Partly decided by how you buy: the terms

Read the detailHide the detail for The Job Slips. The Booking Doesn't.

Accommodation is booked to the programme you had, not the one you end up with. When the job runs long, the rate you agreed in month one is gone by month seven, and you're back in the market, possibly during the local festival.

When the job stops early, the terms bite. Cancelling a long stay after check-in can mean paying for nights nobody uses. That's the exposure line on the receipt, and it's a maximum, not an expectation.

When the crew doubles for six weeks of pours, someone is back on the hours line, starting again.

Distance to Site Is a Safety Variable

Cheaper further out, until it isn't.

Decided by where you put people

Read the detailHide the detail for Distance to Site Is a Safety Variable

The cheapest bed near a site is rarely near the site. It's the house forty minutes out. Every extra twenty minutes each way is forty more minutes in a van, in the dark, on top of the shift.

The cost lands in the accident book, the insurance premium and the mornings someone doesn't turn up, not in the accommodation budget. That's why this line is unpriced on the receipt, and why it's the one that can stop a job.

Rail's own guidance is a useful test for any crew house. It's written for rail, so we use it as a test, not a rule.

07

The Crew That Doesn't Come Back

Where you put them decides who comes back.

Decided by where you put people

Read the detailHide the detail for The Crew That Doesn't Come Back

Fewer people will work away from home, and the ones who do choose employers by where they're put up. Replacing someone costs real money, and most of it is lost output while the replacement gets up to speed.

Where your crew sleeps is one of the few retention levers that already sits in your budget. Their own rooms, a kitchen and somewhere safe for the vans are the things crews notice.

What the Tender Will Ask

If it lives in a spreadsheet, you can't show it.

Decided by how you buy

Read the detailHide the detail for What the Tender Will Ask

Welfare used to be a paragraph. Now it's a weighting, and the requirement flows down the supply chain to the people building the job. The question is no longer "do you have a welfare policy". It's "show us".

If the answer to "where is your crew staying?" is a spreadsheet, a company card and a folder of platform receipts, you have no record of what was vetted, how far it was from site, or whether the smoke alarm worked.

You may have done everything right. You can't show it.

Why this matters now

Why the Bill Is About to Grow

More work, further from home, for fewer people willing to travel.

The work is moving, and it isn't staying local. That was survivable when crews could go home at night. It's less survivable as projects spread to coasts, corridors and grid connections far from the people who build them.

Nobody in the business owns the accommodation number. Some sits on project cost codes, some in expenses, some goes out as a nightly allowance, and some lives on a company card in a drawer in the site office. Nobody adds it up, because adding it up is nobody's job.

Fair questions

Questions You'd Ask Before You Trust It

Are you saying Rooost is cheaper?
No. We can't promise the cheapest nightly rate, and a direct supplier can beat us on it. The point of the receipt is that the nightly rate is only one line. Commission, VAT, booking time and programme terms are decided by how accommodation is bought, and those are the lines we work on.
Where does the platform commission figure come from?
The report assumes a share of each platform-booked night goes to the platform, based on published fee schedules. Fees vary by platform, country and property type, so the calculator starts there and lets you change it. It sits inside the sticker price and is never added to the totals.
Can I reclaim the VAT if I get an invoice?
HMRC says a business can reclaim VAT on accommodation its employees need while working away, if the supplier is VAT registered and you hold a valid VAT invoice addressed to your business. A fixed allowance doesn't qualify. Check your own position with your accountant. This isn't tax advice.
Why does the possible total include a cancellation cost?
It's a maximum, not an expectation. Some long-stay cancellation policies charge for the nights used plus a further stretch after check-in. If your terms don't work that way, set the cancellation tail to zero and the line goes to nothing.
Where does the booker-hours figure come from?
It's an assumption for the worked example: say the booking takes 12 hours a week for that crew. It isn't a measured result, it isn't a client's result and it isn't a promise from us. Change it to your own number. If you don't know your own number, that's the third of the five questions.
Does the calculator store or send my numbers?
No. It runs in your browser, and nothing you type is sent anywhere. The email button opens your own mail app with the numbers filled in. The download form is separate, and it only sends your name, work email and company.
When is a hotel the better call?
Under 4 nights, a hotel will usually be the better call. We do not sell hotel stays. We use a hotel only in an emergency, or when dates do not line up (for example, one night while a property is booked out). Houses start to make sense for stays of 4 nights or more, and for programmes that move.
How current are the figures?
The allowance, hotel rates and platform fees all change. Every source has a date on the sources page, and we say where a figure is old, American or second-hand. Check the publisher for the current number before you quote it in a tender. The source list was compiled on 7 October 2026.

Want to check a figure? Every source is on the sources page, with a plain note on its limits.
